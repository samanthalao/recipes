Shio pan means salt bread in japanese.

Arguably, L’s most popular bread recipe so far. Enjoy!

Dry Ingredients:
4 cups (615 g) bread ****flour
1/4 cup (56 g) white sugar
1/2 tsp (2 g) dough conditioner*
1.5 tbsp (14 g) instant yeast
1 tsp (8 g) salt
1/4 cup (29 g) milk powder**

Wet Ingredients:
1.5 cups (291 g) boiling water
1/4 cup (54 g) neutral oil
1/2 cup (110 g) water

Filling:
2.5 sticks butter, cut into 18 pieces (each stick is cut in half along each dimension). There will be 2 extra sticks.

Topping:
1/4 cup melted butter
1 tbsp kosher salt

Steps:

1. Mix all the dry ingredients together.
2. Mix the boiling water with 197g (1.5c) of the dry ingredients with a spoon or spatula until fully mixed in
3. Add oil, water and the remaining flour mixture.

4. Using a food processor (dough blade) pulse intermittently or stand mixer (dough hook) on low until dough is stretchy (takes about 10 min). Pause the mixing a few minutes in to scrape the bottom/edges to ensure all of the mixture is incorporated.
5. Knead the dough for 2-3 min.

<div style="position: relative; display: inline-block; margin: 10px 0;">
  <img src="/images/shiopan/IMG_8287.jpg" alt="Step 6" style="max-width: 100%; border-radius: 8px;">
  <span style="position: absolute; top: 10px; left: 10px; background: rgba(0,0,0,0.7); color: white; padding: 5px 10px; border-radius: 5px; font-weight: bold;">6</span>
</div>

6. Cover the dough and let rise until tripled in size. The timing for the rise depends on temperature (about 3 hours at 65 F or 1 hour at 100 F).
7. Split the dough into 3 equal parts. For each part, shape the dough into a ball, dust flour on the top and bottom of the ball, then lightly roll out the ball to a 0.5 in thick circle

<div style="position: relative; display: inline-block; margin: 10px 0;">
  <img src="/images/shiopan/IMG_8289.jpg" alt="Step 8" style="max-width: 100%; border-radius: 8px;">
  <span style="position: absolute; top: 10px; left: 10px; background: rgba(0,0,0,0.7); color: white; padding: 5px 10px; border-radius: 5px; font-weight: bold;">8</span>
</div>

8. Cut each circle into 6 equal wedges (so you end up with 18 total wedges).

<div style="position: relative; display: inline-block; margin: 10px 0;">
  <img src="/images/shiopan/IMG_8291.jpg" alt="Step 9" style="max-width: 100%; border-radius: 8px;">
  <span style="position: absolute; top: 10px; left: 10px; background: rgba(0,0,0,0.7); color: white; padding: 5px 10px; border-radius: 5px; font-weight: bold;">9</span>
</div>

9. Dust flour on the sides of each wedge (where you just cut through the dough) to prevent the dough from sticking to the surface or rolling pin. Then roll out the dough to 0.125 in lengthwise. Put a block of butter on the wide side, then roll it up. Wet the inside of the tip of the dough to make it stick.

<div style="position: relative; display: inline-block; margin: 10px 0;">
  <img src="/images/shiopan/IMG_8296.jpg" alt="Step 10" style="max-width: 100%; border-radius: 8px;">
  <span style="position: absolute; top: 10px; left: 10px; background: rgba(0,0,0,0.7); color: white; padding: 5px 10px; border-radius: 5px; font-weight: bold;">10</span>
</div>

10. Let the rolls rise again (1 hr at 65F or half an hour at 100F), generously spraying the dough with water every 30 minutes to prevent the dough from drying out
11. Preheat oven to 450F and put a tray of boiling water on the bottom rack.
12. Put rolls in the oven on the top rack and generously spray water on the bottom of the oven to create more steam.
13. Halfway through baking, generously spray water on the rolls while they are in the oven.
14. Bake until inside of rolls reach 200F, takes about 15 min.
15. Once the bread is out of the oven, brush with the melted butter on the pan that oozed out of the bread & sprinkle with kosher salt on top. Enjoy!

* dough conditioner contains calcium sulfate, L-cystein hydrochloride, vitamin C

**can omit the milk powder and replace the 110g of water with milk

Note: The tray of boiling water on the bottom rack and spraying the oven with water creates steam which makes the bread rise better. Spraying the bread halfway through baking prevents the bread from getting too burnt on the outside.
